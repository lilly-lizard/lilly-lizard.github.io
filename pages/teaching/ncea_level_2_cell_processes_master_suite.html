At low light levels, photolysis of water in the thylakoids slows down, reducing ATP and NADPH supply for the Calvin Cycle.

1. Light-Dependent Phase

Location: Thylakoid Membranes (Grana)
  • Light Absorption: Chlorophyll pigments embedded in thylakoid membranes capture photons of light energy.
  • Photolysis of Water: Solar energy splits water molecules: 2H2O → 4H+ + 4e- + O2. Oxygen gas is released as a metabolic byproduct through stomata.
  • Photophosphorylation: High-energy electrons pass along electron transport chains, driving ATP Synthase to convert ADP + Pi into ATP, and reducing NADP+ into NADPH.
Inputs: H2O, Light Energy, ADP, NADP+
Outputs: O2 (gas byproduct), ATP, NADPH

2. Light-Independent Phase (Calvin Cycle)

Location: Fluid Stroma
  • Carbon Fixation: Carbon dioxide gas diffusing in through stomata is fixed into 3-carbon compounds, catalysed by the enzyme RuBisCO.
  • Reduction Phase: Chemical energy stored in ATP and NADPH from the light-dependent phase is utilised to reduce 3-PGA into Triose Phosphate (G3P).
  • Glucose Synthesis: Triose phosphate molecules exit the cycle and combine to form high-energy Glucose (C6H12O6) or starch for storage.
Inputs: CO2, ATP, NADPH
Outputs: Glucose (C6H12O6), ADP, NADP+

Krebs Cycle

Mitochondrial Matrix

Pyruvate enters matrix and is broken down in a cycle. Releases CO2 gas and produces NADH and FADH2 electron carriers.

Yield: 2 ATP + 6CO2 + NADH

Electron Transport (ETC)

Mitochondrial Cristae

Electrons pass down cristae membrane proteins. Oxygen acts as final electron acceptor, bonding H+ to form H2O.

Yield: 32–34 ATP + 6H2O

Anaerobic Path

Cytoplasm (No O2)

Occurs when oxygen is deficient. Prevents NADH backlog by converting pyruvate to lactic acid (animals) or ethanol (plants/yeast).

Yield: 2 ATP (Fast, low yield)

Comparative Summary: Aerobic vs Anaerobic Respiration (Excellence Key)

Feature Aerobic Respiration Anaerobic Respiration
Oxygen Requirement Required (Final electron acceptor in ETC) Not required
Cellular Location Cytoplasm (Glycolysis) & Mitochondria (Krebs + ETC) Cytoplasm only
Net ATP Yield High (~36–38 ATP per glucose) Very Low (2 ATP per glucose)
End Products Carbon Dioxide (CO2) and Water (H2O) Lactic acid (animals) OR Ethanol & CO2 (yeast/plants)
Biological Purpose & Limitation Sustained energy production for growth, active transport & metabolism. Slower rate. Rapid emergency energy burst during hypoxia. Toxic byproduct buildup causes muscle fatigue.
Cell Division & Molecular Genetics

Cell Cycle, DNA Replication & Mitosis

Understand semi-conservative DNA replication (S phase) and somatic cell division (PMAT + Cytokinesis).

Semi-Conservative DNA Replication Fork

Occurs in the S Phase of Interphase prior to mitosis. Helicase unzips hydrogen bonds; DNA Polymerase builds 5' to 3'.

Leading Strand: Continuous 5' to 3' synthesis toward replication fork by DNA Polymerase.
Lagging Strand: Discontinuous synthesis creating Okazaki fragments joined by Ligase.

Somatic Mitosis Stage Visualiser

Mitosis ensures identical growth and tissue repair by separating sister chromatids into 2 identical diploid (2n) daughter cells.

Prophase: Chromatin condenses into visible chromosomes. The nuclear envelope breaks down and spindle fibres begin to form from centrioles.

Mitochondria, Chloroplasts & Leaf Adaptations

Compare organelle ultrastructure and explore leaf cross-section adaptations for maximised light capture and gas exchange.

Mitochondrion

Aerobic Respiration (36–38 ATP)

Glycolysis: Cytoplasm (2 ATP + Pyruvate)

Krebs Cycle: Fluid Matrix (2 ATP + CO2)

Electron Transport: Folded Cristae (32–34 ATP)

Structural Adaptation: High inner membrane folding (cristae) expands surface area for maximum ETC complexes and ATP Synthase enzymes.

C6H12O6 + 6O2 → 6CO2 + 6H2O + ATP Energy

Chloroplast

Photosynthesis (Glucose)

Light Dependent: Thylakoids/Grana (Water split by solar energy, NADPH made)

Light Independent: Stroma fluid (Calvin cycle fixes CO2 into glucose)

Structural Adaptation: Stacked thylakoid discs (grana) maximize chlorophyll density for photon capture.

6CO2 + 6H2O + Light Energy → C6H12O6 + 6O2

Leaf Cross-Section

Structural Adaptations

Palisade Layer: Tightly packed upper cells rich in chloroplasts for light capture.

Spongy Layer: Loosely packed cells with air spaces facilitating gas diffusion.

Stomata & Guard Cells: Lower epidermis pores regulating CO2/O2 and transpiration.
