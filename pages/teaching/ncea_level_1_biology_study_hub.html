Understanding Genetic Variation

Demonstrate understanding of genetic variation in relation to an identified characteristic. Learn how DNA structure leads to inherited traits, how sexual reproduction and mutation generate variation, and how selective pressures alter population gene pools in Aotearoa New Zealand.

01

DNA, Genes & Chromosomes

Source & Nature of Variation

  • Nucleotides: Sugar, phosphate, nitrogen base (A, T, C, G).
  • Base Pairing: A-T and C-G complementary rules.
  • Triplet Codons: Code for specific amino acids forming functional proteins.
  • Mutations: Permanent DNA base sequence changes creating new alleles.
02

Cell Division & Reproduction

Generating Diversity

  • Meiosis vs Mitosis: 4 unique haploid gametes vs 2 identical diploid body cells.
  • Independent Assortment: Random alignment of homologous chromosome pairs.
  • Crossing Over: Allele segment swap between non-sister chromatids.
  • Random Fertilisation: Any unique sperm + any unique egg.
03

Natural Selection & Gene Pools

Evolution & Survival

  • Selective Pressures: Disease (PSA), predators, environmental shifts.
  • Survival Advantage: Beneficial phenotypes survive & reproduce.
  • Allele Frequency: Favourable alleles increase in population gene pool.
  • Bottleneck Effect: Reduced genetic diversity in small populations.
04

Pedigrees & Monohybrid Crosses

Inheritance Patterns

  • Monohybrid Crosses: 3:1 phenotypic & 1:2:1 genotypic ratios.
  • Pedigree Tracking: Identifying carrier parents of recessive traits.
  • Test Crosses: Crossing with homozygous recessive to find genotype.
  • Probability vs Reality: Why small offspring counts vary from expected ratios.

Topic 1 DNA Structure, Alleles & Heritability

An organism's genetic code is stored inside the nucleus in the form of DNA (Deoxyribonucleic Acid). DNA consists of a double-helix formed by repeating units called nucleotides. Each nucleotide contains a sugar, a phosphate group, and a nitrogenous base (A, T, C, or G).

Mutations: A permanent change in the base sequence of DNA. Mutations are the ultimate source of all new alleles. Somatic mutations affect only the individual, whereas gametic mutations can be inherited by offspring.

Topic 2 Cell Division, Reproduction & Sources of Variation

Meiosis produces 4 genetically unique haploid gametes through independent assortment and crossing over. During random fertilisation, any unique sperm fertilises any unique egg, ensuring offspring are genetically distinct from parents and siblings.

Topic 3 Natural Selection & Gene Pool Dynamics

Selective environmental pressures act on phenotypes. Individuals with favourable phenotypes are more likely to survive and reproduce, passing beneficial alleles to their offspring and increasing the allele frequency in the population gene pool over time.

Topic 4 Pedigree Analysis & Monohybrid Inheritance

Pedigree charts track phenotypes across generations. Heterozygous parents (carriers) can pass recessive alleles to offspring unnoticed until two carriers produce a homozygous recessive child (25% probability).

The IDEL Answer Framework for NCEA Biology

How to structure your written answers to reach Merit and Excellence grades.

1. Identify (Achieved)

Define the basic scientific term or state the direct observation (e.g. "A mutation is a permanent change in the DNA base sequence.")

2. Describe (Achieved+)

Provide details on what happens or what the biological process looks like (e.g. "Mutations in gamete cells can be inherited by offspring.")

3. Explain (Merit)

Explain HOW and WHY it happens using linking words (e.g. "Changing the base order alters triplet codons, which causes a new amino acid order.")

4. Link / Discuss (Excellence)

Connect multiple concepts across generations and tie directly to the question scenario (e.g. "In kiwifruit, survival advantage leads to reproduction, passing the PSA allele...")
