High-Yield Concepts Checklist

Prioritize these three core pillars of AS91157. Almost every NCEA exam has one major long-answer question dedicated to each of these topics. Master the comparisons!

🧬 Topic A: Meiosis & Mutation

The Sources of Variation. Compare and contrast how these create variation.

Mutation

The only source of brand-new alleles in a gene pool.

Crossing Over (Chiasma)

Swapping of genetic material between non-sister chromatids. Creates new combinations of alleles on a chromosome.

Independent Assortment

Random alignment of homologous chromosomes along the equator, mixing maternal and paternal chromosomes.

Segregation

Separating chromatids so each gamete gets only one allele per gene.

👪 Topic B: Patterns of Inheritance

Punnett Squares & Pedigrees. Show clear working and written explanations.

1. Monohybrid Inheritance

Understand the differences between Complete dominance, Co-dominance, Incomplete dominance, Lethal alleles, and Multiple alleles.

2. Dihybrid Crosses (Unlinked)

Predicting offspring phenotypic ratios. Be able to explain why the classic 9:3:3:1 ratio occurs in a heterozygous cross.

3. Linked Genes

Genes located close together on the same chromosome that tend to be inherited together. Crucial: Explain how crossing over can break linkage.

🌍 Topic C: Evolutionary Forces

Gene Pool Change. How allele frequencies change in a population over time.

🦅

Natural Selection

Survival of the fittest. Useful alleles are passed on; harmful ones decrease in frequency due to environmental pressures.

🎲

Genetic Drift

Random changes in allele frequencies, particularly impactful in small populations.

  • Founder Effect: Small group colonizes new area.
  • Bottleneck Effect: Disaster drastically reduces population size.
🚶

Migration (Gene Flow)

Individuals moving in (immigration) or out (emigration), carrying their alleles and altering the gene pool.

The "Excellence Formula"

Many Year 12 students lose marks because they write definitions instead of linked explanations. Use the "Identify, Define, Explain, Link" strategy. Click through the steps below to build an Excellence answer.

Practice Question

"Discuss how the bottleneck effect can lead to a change in the allele frequencies of a small population of NZ Kākāpō."

1

Achieved Level (Define) Click to reveal

Define "bottleneck effect" (a sudden reduction in population size due to an event like disease or human hunting) and define "allele frequency" (how common an allele is in a gene pool).
2

Merit Level (Explain) Click to reveal

Explain that the surviving small population has a random, unrepresentative sample of the original population's alleles, leading to an immediate loss of genetic diversity.
3

Excellence Level (Link) Click to reveal

Link this to the future survival of the species. Explain that because genetic variation is low, if the environment changes (e.g., a new disease introduced), the Kākāpō population is highly vulnerable because they lack the diverse alleles required for some individuals to survive, potentially leading to extinction.
