Wildlife Forensic Analysis

The Longfin Tuna (Eel) Poaching Case Study

1 The Case of the Fiordland Longfin Eel Poaching

Rangers in Fiordland National Park have discovered a cooler filled with suspected wild-harvested Longfin Eels (Kōkopu/Tuna). Longfin eels are protected, and harvesting them from national parks is strictly illegal.

Three fish-processing plants in the South Island were found with undocumented eel shipments. You must identify if the eels in the cooler match the genetic profile of any of the shipments from the three suspect processors.

The Enzyme Instruction:

Your restriction enzyme digests DNA whenever it runs into the pattern: G G C C. It makes its cut down the center: GG | CC.

2 Digital Scissors: DNA Restriction Cutter Simulator

How to cut your DNA online:
  1. Scan each sequence to find the G G C C patterns.
  2. Hover your cursor between the letters G and C (down the middle of the pattern).
  3. Click on the gap to make a slice. A red scissor line (✂️) will appear to mark your cut!
  4. Count the base letters in each segment you have created, and type the numbers in the boxes in Section 3.

4 Analysis & Evaluation (NCEA Style Assessment)

Question 1: Overall Findings (Conclusion)

State which suspect shipment matches the poached Fiordland longfin eels. Justify your conclusion using specific evidence from your completed gel board, including exact fragment base-pair (bp) sizes and band placements.

Question 2: Investigation Type & Data Collected

Identify the name of this specific scientific investigation approach. Describe what this investigation type involves, and explain the different kinds of data (such as qualitative phenotypic traits versus binary, molecular-level DNA data) that were collected throughout this process.

Question 3: Evaluation (Why or Why Not is this Investigation Type Useful?)

Evaluate why or why not this investigation type (Classifying and Identifying) is useful for resolving biological cases. In your answer, include:

  • The biological strengths (certainty, structural tracking) and limitations (lack of cause-and-effect, convergent evolution/misleading physical features) of this method;
  • Whether or not physical traits and DNA tracking support each other or can contradict each other;
  • How this investigation type can work together with other approaches (such as chemical testing or pattern seeking) to give you a highly confident, integrated understanding of an identity.
